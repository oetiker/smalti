# Smalti 8x16 — design

Status: draft for review
Date: 2026-08-25
Target release: 0.2.0

## 1. What ships

A complete second cell size, `Smalti 8x16`, at full parity with `Smalti 7x14`:
four faces, the same 1004-codepoint target set, no missing glyphs, its own
`make check` green, and both sizes in one `.deb` and one `.rpm`.

Parity is the requirement, not an aspiration.  A partial 8x16 does not ship.

## 2. Why 8x16

Upstream Tamzen offers 5x9, 6x12, 7x13, 7x14, 8x15, 8x16 and 10x20.  Of those,
**only 6x12, 7x14, 8x16 and 10x20 are exactly 1:2** (see the aspect-ratio note
in `2026-08-21-glyph-store-design.md:291`).  Because all four share one shape,
moving between any two of them is a *uniform* scale — 7x14 to 8x16 is x8/7 in
both axes, inserting one column in every seven and one row in every seven with
no distortion.

Every non-1:2 target squashes.  7x14 to 8x15 would be x1.143 wide against
x1.071 tall, and a squashed draft is worse than a blank grid because it looks
nearly right.

8x16 is chosen over 6x12 because it is the smallest 1:2 step *up*: the two
extra rows buy headroom for a mark above a capital, which is a standing pain at
7x14, and 8x16 is the terminal cell users ask for next.

The cost is honest and named in §5: 7 columns have a true centre column, 8 do
not, so every horizontally symmetric glyph needs a bias decision.

## 3. The three layers

`docs/coverage.md` gives the exact split per face at 7x14:

| layer | regular | where it comes from |
|---|---:|---|
| upstream | 189 | `upstream/7x14/Tamzen7x14{r,b}.bdf` |
| hand | 309 | `glyphs/7x14/regular/*.txt` |
| generated | 506 | the seven generators, into `build/gen/7x14/` |
| **total** | **1004** | |

All three must reach 8x16.  The generated layer is comparable work to the hand
layer and must not be under-counted.

### 3.1 Upstream layer — near-free

Vendor `upstream/8x16/Tamzen8x16r.bdf` and `Tamzen8x16b.bdf` from the same
Tamzen release the 7x14 files came from.  This yields ~189 glyphs and, more
importantly, the bias evidence of §5.

### 3.2 Generated layer — three generators must be re-authored

Three generators refuse any size but 7x14, on purpose and loudly:

    gen-braille.py:26         'the dot geometry below is 7x14 only'
    gen-arrows.py:31          'the parts below are drawn at 7x14'
    gen-circled-digits.py:31  'the ring below is 7x14 only'

Those guards are correct behaviour and **stay**.  Each gains a second accepted
size with its own geometry beside the 7x14 geometry — never a scaled
approximation of it.  Braille is 256 glyphs on its own; at 8 columns the
two-by-four dot arrangement has room it did not have at 7.

`gen-latin-ext-a.py` composes against the upstream BDF it is handed and is
expected to work unchanged; this must be confirmed, not assumed.

Two tuned constants:

* `tools/weight.py:21` — `CELL_W = 7`, the emboldening rule, shared with the
  bold oblique.  Becomes a parameter.
* `tools/slant-bdf.py:65` — `STEPS = (5, 8)`, tuned to spend 7x14's side
  bearings, with an explicit exit when the lean overruns them.  8x16 needs its
  own lean.  `CELL_W` there is already an overridable global (`slant-bdf.py:110`).

### 3.3 Hand layer — 309 drawings, regular only

`glyphs/8x16/regular/` receives 309 drawings.  Bold derives through
`embolden.py`, the obliques through the shear, exactly as at 7x14.  Per-face
overrides remain available and are expected to be rare.

## 4. Draft strategy — by glyph class, not one scale

**A single x8/7 nearest-neighbour scale is the right arithmetic and the wrong
operation.**  Inserting one column into seven means some 1-pixel stem crossing
the duplicated column becomes 2 pixels while every other stem stays 1.
Inconsistent stem weight is the most visible defect a bitmap face can carry.

**Padding is wrong too, and measurement is why.**  Comparing the two upstream
files glyph by glyph: 7x14's letters occupy columns 1..5, and 8x16's occupy
columns 1..6, with wide ones (`M T W X Y % +`) reaching column 7.  Of 23
symmetric glyphs, **18 changed ink width**; only 3 kept their width and moved
right one column.  Upstream did not shift its letters into the new column — it
made them wider.  A padded 7x14 glyph would sit visibly thin beside them.

Measuring Smalti's own 309 drawings shows the font already carries two
conventions: **150 span the full cell** (columns 0..6) and **159 sit in a letter box** (starting at column 1, most ending at column 5).

**The class is therefore computed, not judged.**  A 7x14 drawing that lights
both column 0 and column 6 is cell-spanning; anything else is letter-box.  This
is derivable from the existing file, so no glyph can be misfiled by opinion.

| class | measured count | strategy |
|---|---:|---|
| **cell-spanning** — box drawing, rules, blocks, joining arrows | 150 | **extend to fill columns 0..7.**  Never pad: a horizontal rule drawn to column 6 leaves column 7 blank and the rule comes apart at every cell boundary.  That is a broken glyph, not a narrow one |
| **letter-box** — Greek, punctuation, math, dingbats, standalone arrows | 159 | **widen** into the new letter box (columns 1..6, wide forms to 7), matching what upstream did to its own letters |
| **solid shapes** — a subset of the above, filled forms with no stems | small | **scale** x8/7 within whichever box its class gives it |

The stem rule of §5.2 governs placement inside either box: a single centred
vertical stem sits at column 4.  A box-drawing vertical is cell-spanning in
height and still stem-placed in width; the two rules compose rather than
compete.

Greek cannot be derived.  A Greek letter must sit beside upstream's Latin and
read as the same typeface; at 8x16 those neighbours are different drawings, so
a scaled 7x14 alpha would sit wrong next to an 8x16 `a` however well it scaled.

A draft is a starting point, never an output.  Every drawing is judged before
it enters `glyphs/8x16/`.

### 4.1 The prize in the extra room

`README.md` records honest limits at 7x14: a wave arrow and a squiggle arrow
differ by one pixel of amplitude, and a triple arrow is three lines because
four would touch.  Several such compromises stop being necessary at 8x16.
Padding yields a correct glyph; the extra room permits a better one.  Where a
7x14 compromise can be lifted, lift it and note it in the batch review.

## 5. The bias rule

### 5.1 Vertical is determined, not chosen

Our glyphs must sit on the same baseline as upstream's letters.  The placement
of the two extra rows is therefore read directly from
`upstream/8x16/Tamzen8x16r.bdf` — its `FONTBOUNDINGBOX`, `FONT_ASCENT` and
`FONT_DESCENT`.  For reference, 7x14 is `FONTBOUNDINGBOX 7 14 0 -3`,
`FONT_ASCENT 11`, `FONT_DESCENT 3`.

No decision to make.  Record the 8x16 values in `glyphs/8x16/README.md`.

### 5.2 Horizontal is chosen once, from upstream

At 7 wide, column 3 is a true centre (3 left, 3 right).  At 8 wide there is
none: a stem at column 3 leaves 3 left and 4 right; at column 4, the reverse.

Read the convention off upstream 8x16's own symmetric glyphs — `|`, `+`, `^`,
`~`, `<`, `>`, `(`, `)` — and adopt whatever they do.  It is **one rule applied
to all 309**, not a per-glyph lookup.  Only 17 of the 309 drawings are at or
below U+00FF, so upstream cannot answer glyph-for-glyph; it does not need to.

**Open risk:** upstream 8x16 may not be self-consistent.  If its `|` and its
`+` disagree, there is no single rule to read and we choose one and document
the reasoning.  Settling this is the first task of implementation and costs
minutes.

The rule is written into `glyphs/8x16/README.md`, which is the per-size grid
reference and the first thing a contributor reads.

## 6. The comparison chart

`make compare` builds a self-contained page at `build/compare/index.html`.

* Each glyph is one tile: **7x14 at 28 px beside 8x16 at 32 px**.  Both are
  exact 2x multiples, so both are pixel-crisp and neither blurs, and the two
  come out at nearly the same physical height — which is what makes a
  like-for-like judgement possible.
* Every tile is labelled with its codepoint, so a reviewer can name the ones
  that need work.
* The page `@font-face`s the built `.woff2` out of `build/`, so it needs
  nothing installed and always shows the current build.
* Tiles are grouped by Unicode block, in the drawing-batch order of §7.

**`make compare` is not a check.**  `make check` proves things; `make compare`
shows things.  It is never a prerequisite of `check`, and it never gates
anything.  Keeping the two apart matters in this project.

## 7. Workflow

Drawing proceeds in Unicode-block batches, largest first, because a block is
the natural batch and the house rule is "draw a batch, render it as a strip,
then judge":

| batch | count |
|---|---:|
| `27xx` dingbats, arrows | 147 |
| `03xx` Greek | 49 |
| `21xx` arrows, letterlike | 21 |
| `25xx` box drawing, shapes | 19 |
| `22xx` math | 17 |
| `20xx` punctuation | 17 |
| `00xx` Latin-1 | 17 |
| Powerline, technical, misc | 22 |
| **total** | **309** |

Per batch: draw and self-judge every glyph, regenerate `make compare`, hand the
chart to the user, receive the list of glyphs that need changing, fix, repeat
until the batch is accepted.

Division of labour is explicit.  Drawing and first judgement are the
implementer's.  The comparison against 7x14 and the ruling on what needs
changing are the user's.

## 8. Build changes

* `Makefile:39` — `SIZE := 7x14` becomes `SIZES := 7x14 8x16`, and the ~26
  size-dependent references become per-size targets.  This is the one
  structurally invasive edit.
* Per-size targets must keep each size's `check` able to go red on its own.
* `make check` runs per size.  8x16 must go green independently before it
  ships.
* `tools/build-face.py`, `check-site.py`, `test-check-glyphs.py` and
  `slant-bold.py` already take a size argument and need no interface change.
* `docs/coverage.md` already states "the union of everything any size covers",
  so `glyph-index.py` is built for this and gains a second row group.

## 9. Packaging, site, docs

* `packaging/nfpm.yaml:55-62` carries eight `.ttf` instead of four.  One
  package holds every cell size — already settled, and safe only because the
  size is in the family name.
* `tools/check-packages.py` proves all eight are byte-identical to the ones
  `make check` validated.
* The specimen site renders both sizes.
* `README.md` gains 8x16 throughout, and the "Only the 7x14 size is drawn"
  known-gap note (`README.md:873`) is corrected.
* `glyphs/8x16/README.md` is written: the grid reference, the recorded
  vertical metrics of §5.1 and the horizontal bias rule of §5.2.
* `CHANGES.md` gains the 8x16 entry under `## [Unreleased]`, `### New`.

## 10. What must be true before it ships

* `make check` green for both sizes, independently.
* `make check-site` green.
* `make check-packages` green with eight fonts.
* `docs/coverage.md` shows 8x16 with **0 missing** in all four faces.
* Both packages byte-reproducible, as today.
* Every one of the 309 has been through a chart review and been accepted.

## 11. Open decisions — the user's to make

1. **The oblique lean at 8x16.**  `STEPS` must be chosen for the new side
   bearings.  Brought as a rendered comparison, never as a quietly picked
   number.

   **DECIDED 2026-08-25 — the lean is 2 PIXELS**, the same total displacement
   as 7x14's `STEPS = (5, 8)`.  Exactly two entries in `STEPS`; a third entry
   makes it a 3-pixel lean and is ruled out.  Recorded here because a reviewer
   correctly refused to take a controller's word for it — a decision that lives
   only in a session transcript cannot be checked by anyone later.

   Which two rows carry the step remains open, and is judged from rendered
   output rather than from the numbers.
2. **Whether to re-tune the emboldening trade.**  The 108-vs-93
   fidelity-for-legibility choice (`tools/weight.py:5,11`; `README.md:754`) was
   deliberate at 7x14.
   8x16 has more room, so the trade may land differently.  Default is to keep
   the 7x14 behaviour and change nothing unless the chart argues otherwise.

## 12. Risks

1. **Upstream 8x16 is internally inconsistent on bias.**  §5.2.  Settled in
   minutes at the start; would cost a documented judgement call, not rework.
2. **The generated layer is larger than it looks.**  506 glyphs per face, of
   which Braille alone is 256.  Named here so it is not discovered late.
3. **A draft that looks nearly right is worse than none.**  §4.  The mitigation
   is that no draft enters `glyphs/8x16/` unjudged, and every glyph faces the
   chart.
4. **Doc drift.**  This project has a logged history of true-when-written
   sentences that the code later contradicted.  Every 7x14-specific claim in
   `README.md`, the Makefile header and `packaging/nfpm.yaml` must be re-read
   against the code when 8x16 lands, not against memory.

## 13. Non-goals

* No third cell size.  The remaining five stay undrawn and the layout keeps
  accommodating them.
* No variable font and no `opsz` axis.  One file per strike stands.
* No apt or yum repository.  That would need a signing key.
* No change to the 7x14 drawings.  8x16 must not perturb the existing face;
  the byte-reproducible build is the instrument that proves it did not.
